Draft: evidence

A claim needs its receipt.

Method, result, limits and the next check go together. That way a page stays useful while the answer is still changing.

Display class

Labels for public, aggregated, synthetic and unverified material. No performance promises.

Status labels

Say how you know.

"Verified" means the command and the output are named. "Unverified" isn't an apology. It tells you where the checking stopped.

How a receipt works

Question to next check.

Question, observation, limits, next check. The open question is still there at the end.

Public

A named site, an approved screenshot, or a public method page you can look at.

Fixture

A repeatable example with known inputs. Good for showing a rule or a fallback.

Local lab

A render or interaction check in a named mode. It's still only a local result.

Independent

A second check of the same thing, with its own method and limits.

Open

A known gap, next check, or rights question that isn't settled.

Private

Exact data, identifiers, private chats and operational details. Kept off the site.

Cross-project receipt

Failures go in the table too.

A draft, based on the project notes. Every re-check comes with a sentence saying what was actually tested and what wasn't.

The rule

If a limit matters, show it.

Put a real table or a text description next to every chart. Say whether a value is public, aggregated, synthetic or private. Keep the date and the limits close to the number.

What this does not prove
A public receipt isn't a native Tester result, broker result, live result, deployment result, promotion decision or legal privacy clearance.

Question

What was the work trying to show?

Observation

Limitation
